Built like the data matters.

Because it does.

Sealed before it touches disk

Your password derives a 512-bit key through PBKDF2-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations. AES-256-GCM encrypts the vault. Brute force faces rate-limited lockout with exponential backoff.

The key stays in your head

Nothing to recover, nothing to leak. The stored verifier only proves the right password was typed — the AES key itself is derived fresh, non-extractable, and lives in memory for seconds at a time.

Encrypted sync, done right

Mirrors through Chrome/Brave Sync as ciphertext chunks — merge without ever exposing entries. Your bookmarks follow you across devices; your password does not.

Docked side panel

Open the vault where you browse — pinned to the right of the window. The "Bookmark this page" row follows your active tab live, one click to seal the page you're reading.

Never locked in

Export your whole vault to standard Netscape HTML — importable back into Chrome and Brave bookmarks, any day. No walled garden, no hostage-taking.

Open source, auditable

Every line of crypto and sync lives in a public repo you can read in an afternoon. No obfuscation, no black box, no "trust us."

I understand — let's seal it

Fair questions, straight answers.

Can the developer read my bookmarks?

No. There is no backend, no API, nothing calling home. The data exists only inside your browser profile — encrypted. The code that does it is public.

What does Sync actually send?

Only ciphertext: salt, verifier, IV and encrypted chunks. Chrome/Brave Sync streams that between your devices; nobody along the way — including your browser vendor — sees a single bookmark in the clear.

I forgot my password. Now what?

The vault is unrecoverable — permanently. That is by design: anything that could recover it could also be stolen. Reinstall to start fresh.

Does it slow my browser down?

The key-stretching work runs once, at unlock. Browsing, saving and searching are instant.
